Our History

Seven Decades of Sanitary Engineering

AIDIS was born alongside the World Health Organization in 1948 — and has been the technical conscience of water and sanitation in the Americas ever since.

The Inter-American Association of Sanitary and Environmental Engineering (Asociación Interamericana de Ingeniería Sanitaria y Ambiental — AIDIS) is a non-profit, technical-scientific civil society. It gathers the leading institutions of professionals and students across the three Americas who work in environmental preservation, health, and sanitation.

Its founding on 14 April 1948 marked a turning point for the sector: created just seven days after the founding of the World Health Organization, AIDIS has maintained broad collaboration with the WHO and the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), ensuring recognition and representation in their assemblies and executive committees.

1948

Founded in the Americas

AIDIS is established on 14 April 1948, seven days after the World Health Organization — beginning a lasting partnership with the WHO and PAHO.

20th Century

A Continent-Wide Network

National sections are formed across the Americas, uniting engineers, scientists and public-health professionals around shared water and sanitation goals.

Permanent Seat

The Abel Wolman Headquarters

AIDIS's permanent seat — named for the pioneering sanitation engineer Abel Wolman — is located in São Paulo, where its activities are coordinated.

Today

24 National Sections

AIDIS today spans 24 national sections and roughly 32,000 members, coordinated through its technical divisions, with a footprint touching more than 32 countries of the Americas and the Caribbean.
